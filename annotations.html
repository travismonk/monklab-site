Features

Annotations

Many browser extensions let you write notes on webpages. Byblos is not really about writing notes. It is about what happens after you write them.

Normally, your thoughts and the pages they refer to drift apart. You save a page in one place, leave a note somewhere else, discuss it in a chat application, and eventually forget how those pieces fit together. Months later, you might find the page but not remember why it mattered, or find the note and have no idea what it was referring to.

Byblos keeps those things together. Notes, ratings, reactions, measurements, discussions, and reminders all live directly on the pages they describe. The page becomes more than a URL. It becomes a record of what you and your collaborators thought about it.

Annotations Become Navigation

This becomes more useful as your workspace grows. A rating is not just a rating. A note is not just a note. They become part of how you navigate the workspace.

The tree can show information from annotations without requiring you to open every page. Maybe you don't remember where a page is in the tree, but you do remember that you left a heart and a rating on it. Suddenly that page is easy to find. You can also sort and filter pages using the information you have recorded. Pages that would otherwise disappear into a pile of bookmarks become easier to find because you left context behind.

Chats about a webpage should be on the webpage

Most conversations about webpages happen somewhere else. Someone sends a link in a chat application and the discussion becomes separated from the page being discussed. A few weeks later, nobody remembers which conversation belonged to which page.

Byblos keeps the conversation on the page itself. When you return to the page, the discussion is still there. When you share the page with someone else, the discussion comes with it.

The Big Idea

Annotations stop being isolated notes and start becoming part of your browsing environment. They help you remember why a page mattered, help you find it again later, and make it easier to share your thinking with other people.

A note by itself is useful. A note that changes how you navigate, organize, discover, and discuss pages is much more powerful.
