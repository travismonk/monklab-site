Sharing Workspaces

Byblos is useful when you work alone, but it becomes much more interesting when you work with other people.

Most collaboration around webpages is surprisingly fragmented. Someone sends a link in a chat application. Someone else bookmarks it. Another person writes notes in a document. A fourth person responds in a different conversation entirely. Eventually the pages and the context become separated from one another.

A shared Byblos workspace keeps those things together. Pages, annotations, discussions, ratings, measurements, and decisions all live in the same place. Instead of sending people a collection of links and hoping they understand the surrounding context, you can share the workspace itself.

This works particularly well when a group of people are trying to make a decision together. Travel planning, shopping, research projects, work investigations, and family decisions all involve collecting pages, discussing them, comparing them, and eventually choosing between them. Byblos was built around that process.

Sharing Is Simple

Sharing a workspace is intentionally simple. When you click the share button, Byblos copies a block of text to your clipboard. You can send that text using whatever communication tools you already use.

The shared text contains everything needed to join the workspace, including installation instructions for people who do not already have Byblos installed. The recipient installs the extension, pastes the code, and immediately gains access to the workspace.

There are no accounts to create, no passwords to remember, and no onboarding process beyond installing the extension and pasting the invite.

Access and Ownership

Byblos does not require sign-ups, email addresses, social media accounts, payment information, or a central identity provider.

Access is based entirely on possession of the workspace invite. Anyone who receives the invite can access the workspace. For now, this means that you and your collaborators are responsible for deciding who should receive access. If you send someone the invite, you are granting them access to that workspace.

Before sharing a workspace, take a moment to make sure it contains only pages you intend to share. Shared workspaces are designed for collaboration, and collaborators should be people you trust.

Sharing Options

Sometimes you want to share an entire workspace. Other times you only want to share the pages you personally worked on.

Byblos supports both approaches. You can share your complete tree, or you can create a share that contains only the pages that pass your filters. The information buttons next to the sharing controls explain exactly what each sharing mode includes.

The Big Idea

The internet already makes it easy to share pages. What it does not do particularly well is help people make decisions about those pages together.

Byblos is a social browser. It allows people to collect webpages, organize them, discuss them, annotate them, and eventually reach conclusions together. Instead of sharing links, you share the context around those links. Instead of sharing destinations, you share the journey that led there.
